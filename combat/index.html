Steel, stealth, and everything in between

Combat

Fights are close, loud and decided by position. Everything else is a way to avoid one.

Melee

Lock-on, defence, finishing

A cinematic orbit camera locks to a target and switches by a flick of the stick. You can block, dodge-roll, and, with a shield, parry: strike inside the attacker’s glowing window and they stagger instead of you.

Hold the heavy attack. Finish a weakened enemy with an execution and the camera frames the kill. Elite enemies enter a second, faster phase at half health. Bosses punish habit.

Every weapon family has a signature technique on its own button, with a shared cooldown, and every one has a mastery perk to sharpen it. Enemies use the same techniques against you.

Weapon techniques

Each weapon has a secret

Dagger

Flurry

Eight quick stabs. Quiet, cruel, close.

Sword

Sundering Thrust

A lunge that ignores part of an enemy’s armor.

Axe

Rending Cleave

Strikes up to three enemies and leaves them bleeding.

Greatsword

Executioner’s Arc

A wide frontal sweep.

Greataxe

Falling Ruin

Leap, and break the ground where you land.

Bow

Arrowstorm

Five waves of arrows fall on an aimed spot.

Unarmed

Savage Combination

A four-hit combination for when it comes to that.

Torch

Torch Sweep

A sweeping strike with a lit torch. Its burning effects are still in development.

Other techniques are learned from people in the world, such as the Thieves’ Gutter Cut, a dagger strike that leaves a wound that will not close.

Not everything is a fight

Other ways through

Stealth

Crouch. Stay out of the light. A detection meter reads distance, light, your skill and the angle of view. Throw a stone. Pick a pocket. Pick a lock. Sleeping people stay asleep, until they do not.

Taming

A goat, a deer, a moose, a cow, a bear or even a Minotaur can be tamed. Tamed animals follow, fight, level and, within a window, can be revived.

Dirty tricks

Pocket sand, bear traps and rope snares. Not honourable. Very effective.

Together

Co-op

The campaign supports up to four players. Host a game on your network, join by address, or share one PC with two-player split-screen; couch players can also join an online session. A PvP arena hub supports up to eight, in team matches from 1v1 to 4v4. More about the Arena.

Every player keeps their own saves, quests, karma and law record. Party members cannot damage each other.

Online play currently covers LAN and direct-IP connections. Internet play through a service such as Steam has not been enabled yet.

Controls

Controller and keyboard

Every flow in the game is built for gamepad as well as mouse and keyboard. A Relief can be thrown with a quick tap; spells are chosen with shoulder-button chords.
